Local canopy data: Two 2022 canopy-cover rasters for the San José and San Francisco–Oakland urban areas supply mapped crowns within the CA-17 clips. Two county height rasters supply Santa Clara 2020 vegetation-only heights and Alameda–Contra Costa heights from mixed survey years. These supplement existing mapped trees, building shadows and WRI/Meta canopy evidence for walking and cycling.

QGIS preparation: QGIS was used to overlay canopy on satellite imagery, check alignment and resolution, and clip canopy/height layers to CA-17. EcoTrace uses these prepared inputs to calculate route scores.

Dataset counts: Four local rasters: two canopy-cover layers and two height layers. Open this section to load the installed model and valid canopy-pixel counts.

Route averages: Each route is divided into sections at most 15 m long and sampled at each section's midpoint. Longer sections carry more weight in the route average; at constant speed this also weights by travel time. The average represents samples along the route.

Shadow method: Sun rays are checked every 0.6 m up to 120 m toward the sun, from a 1.5 m sampling height. The model assumes flat local ground, vertical vegetation columns and a 65/100 canopy contribution when a ray is blocked. The higher of the raster and existing tree contributions is used, then building shadows and the heat adjustment contribute to the final index.

Walking comfort: With complete nearby weather, a sunny radiation scenario estimates mean radiant temperature (MRT), then UTCI uses MRT, air temperature, humidity and wind. MRT/UTCI are averaged across route sections; the hottest sampled UTCI and estimated minutes at UTCI ≥32°C are also reported. Station weather and departure-time shade stay fixed during the trip. Otherwise, the heat-map model supplies averaged heat scores and estimated sun/shade minutes. These are modeled conditions, not measured sidewalk or body temperatures.

Height gaps: Canopy pixels without usable height, or with unconfirmed height units, use a labeled 8 m assumption. Gaps can result from clipped coverage, NoData, invalid values, or differences between survey dates and mapped crowns. The cause of an individual gap is not established by a missing value alone. An unknown unit means the height number exists but its scale is not configured. Zero height remains zero; missing canopy pixels remain unknown.

Height coverage: Counts load when this section opens. They describe canopy ray samples checked since server startup, including repeated pixels and diagnostic probes. They are not a district-wide missing-height percentage.

Limitations: Crown and height surveys may be from different years. Canopy attenuation and crown geometry are approximations, and long shadows are truncated at 120 m. Time-matched observations are needed to assess accuracy. Route choice still uses modeled shade and detour limits.
